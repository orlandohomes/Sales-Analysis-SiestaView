The two facts that set this price

The absence of a pool and the presence of a pond pull in opposite directions. Both are measurable in this data set.

No pool is the larger of the two adjustments. Four of the five homes that closed in Villa Sol this year had a private pool. The one that did not, 2806 Via Largo Court, is the same home that launched at $534,000, was cut to $499,000, sat 189 days without a contract, canceled, and eventually closed at $485,000 with a $10,000 seller credit attached. Every fast sale in this neighborhood had a pool. In this submarket a screened in-ground pool is contributing somewhere between $25,000 and $35,000 to value, and just as importantly, it is contributing speed.

The pond view is a genuine and scarce asset. The two homes on Riachuelo Lane that back to the pond sold at 100 percent and 99.5 percent of asking price, one of them in twenty days and the other in two. They are the strongest sale-to-list results in the entire study. Only one active listing in Villa Sol currently offers a water view, and the marketing on 3129 Riachuelo leaned on exactly this feature: no rear neighbors, just sky, water, and trees.

Water recovers part of what the missing pool costs. It does not recover all of it.

Villa Sol is a two-speed market. Homes priced inside the proven range go under contract in two to four weeks at close to full asking price. Homes priced above it do not sell slowly, they simply do not sell. Six of the eleven listings that concluded in the past year expired, canceled, or were withdrawn after an average of 111 days.

With roughly eight months of standing inventory, buyers have leverage and time. Price is doing almost all of the work here, and for a home without a pool it is doing even more.

3044 Camino Real is the most instructive listing on the board

It is the only other five bedroom home without a pool in the active set. It has been on the market 119 days, was reduced from $489,000 to $469,900, and still has no contract, and it carries fully paid solar panels that the listing claims save the owner $350 to $400 a month. If a five bedroom with that kind of running-cost advantage cannot find a buyer at $469,900, the subject cannot be launched above it. Coming in at $464,900 places the home underneath it with 444 more square feet and one more full bath.

2986 Siesta View Drive is the same floor plan, three doors away

It came on the market this morning at $499,000 with identical heated square footage, one more bedroom, the same four full baths, a screened pool with an in-ground spa, and a first-floor in-law suite. It will be the first thing a buyer compares your home against.

At $464,900 the subject undercuts it by $34,100, which is a wider gap than the pool is worth and gives buyers a clear reason to look at your home first. The two listings will also help each other. Buyers who tour 2986 and decide they do not want to maintain a pool land directly on your doorstep, and the pond view is the answer to what they give up.

The other pressure point is 2807 Via Largo Court, a 3,140 square foot, five bedroom home owned by the Secretary of Veterans Affairs at $401,000. It is sold as is with no seller disclosures, which explains 69 days on market. It will not appraise as a true comparable, but it appears in every buyer search next to your listing and drags the perceived ceiling for large Villa Sol homes downward. Expect buyer agents to bring it up.

The cautionary story: 2806 Via Largo Court

This is the closest thing Villa Sol has to a controlled experiment on overpricing, and it happens to be the only no-pool sale in the set. It launched at $534,000, was reduced to $499,000, sat 189 days without a contract, canceled, and eventually closed at $485,000 with a $10,000 seller credit. The seller spent nine months and roughly $59,000 in price and credits to arrive at the number the market would have paid at the start.

Note that two doors down on the same street, 2966 Siesta View canceled after 49 days at $460,000 and 2925 Siesta View was withdrawn at $484,900 without ever accumulating a day of market time. Siesta View sellers have been testing the high $400s repeatedly and coming away empty.

Why 30 to 60 days rather than the 19 day neighborhood median

Three factors stretch the expected timeline beyond what the raw median suggests.

  • No pool. Every home that contracted in under 30 days had one. In a Kissimmee gated community marketed heavily to families and to investors, the pool is often the deciding feature, and its absence removes a slice of the buyer pool immediately.
  • Size. The quick sales were all between 1,970 and 2,338 square feet, which is the heart of the Villa Sol buyer pool. At 2,708 square feet with five bedrooms, fewer buyers need this much house.
  • Inventory. Nine active listings against roughly one sale a month means buyers are in no hurry.

Working the other way, the pond view produced the two best sale-to-list results in the study and one two-day contract. Photographed well, it is the feature most likely to compress that 30 to 60 day window toward the low end.

Strategy and things to watch

  • Launch at $464,900 and hold it for 21 days. The first three weeks generate the best traffic the listing will ever see. If there are no offers by day 21 with showings coming through, reduce to $454,900. If showings themselves are thin, the price is too high and the correction needs to be larger.
  • Sell the water, not the square footage. The pond view is the feature that produced a two-day contract and two full-price sales on Riachuelo. Photograph the rear elevation at sunrise or in late afternoon light, and lead the photo order with it rather than burying it at frame twelve. The phrase to use is no rear neighbors, because that is what buyers are actually paying for.
  • Answer the pool question before it is asked. Buyers will compare directly against 2986 Siesta View. Get a rough contractor estimate for adding a screened pool on this lot and have it available. A buyer who learns they can add a pool for a known number, on a pond lot, at $34,100 less than the house down the street, has a decision to make instead of an objection.
  • Position against 3044 Camino Real deliberately. It is the closest rival at $469,900 with 444 fewer square feet and one fewer full bath, and it has been sitting 119 days. Pricing $5,000 below it makes the comparison obvious to every buyer agent in the market.
  • Confirm the CDD balance before launch. Annual CDD fees across these comparables run from $1,200 to $4,941. A high assessment cuts into a buyer's qualifying power and needs to be disclosed with the addendum attached rather than surfacing during inspection.
  • Use a closing cost credit before cutting the price a second time. Two recent Villa Sol sellers closed with credits, including $10,000 at 2806 Via Largo. A credit moves the buyer while preserving the reported sale price for the next comparable.
  • Market to investors as well as families. Villa Sol permits short-term rentals and several listings advertise it. Five bedrooms and four full baths on a pond lot works well for that buyer, and it widens a pool that the missing swimming pool narrows.
